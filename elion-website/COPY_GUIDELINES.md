# Elion : Copywriting Guidelines

> Living document. Every headline, paragraph, button label, and email goes through this checklist.
> When in doubt, the rule is **plain language wins over clever language.**

---

## 0. Positioning & Hierarchy (read first)

### 0.1 Positioning statement

> **We help growing businesses do more with the team they already have by designing automation, custom software, and AI systems that remove operational friction.**

Every page, section, and headline reinforces this statement or it gets cut.

### 0.2 Messaging hierarchy (every section follows this order)

1. **Business problem**: name the visitor's pain first.
2. **Operational outcome**: what changes when the pain goes away.
3. **Solution**: what Elion actually does.
4. **Technology**: only after the value is clear; briefly, supporting the story.
5. **Next step**: the CTA.

Technology supports the story. It never leads it.

**Test:** If you remove every mention of "AI," does the section still make sense? If yes, the technology is in the right place. If no, the technology is leading.

### 0.3 Default vocabulary

Use **systems, operations, workflows, business processes, software** as the default language. Use **AI** sparingly: only when AI is specifically what makes the system work.

The visitor should understand the value before they encounter the word "AI."

## 1. Brand Voice

### 1.1 If Elion were speaking

> Elion is the kind of person who explains a complex thing simply. Who says "I don't know" when they don't know. Who'd rather give you a real answer than a polished one. Who treats your time as valuable. Who tells you what an engagement will cost only after they understand what you're trying to do: and would rather say "this isn't a fit" than take the work.

That's the voice. Every piece of copy should sound like that person wrote it.

### 1.2 The voice in three rules

1. **Direct, not clever.** A button says "Book a Discovery Call," not "Let's Talk."
2. **Specific, not general.** "Reports take 3 days to compile" beats "reporting is slow."
3. **Honest, not boastful.** Show the work. Let the reader decide the verdict.

## 2. Writing Style

### 2.1 Sentence-level

- **Average sentence length: 12-18 words.** Long sentences are fine if they're structured; most should be short.
- **Active voice.** "We design systems" not "systems are designed."
- **Strong verbs.** Replace "is able to" with "can." Replace "make use of" with "use."
- **No subordinate clauses when a period will do.** Two short sentences > one tangled one.
- **Avoid the passive voice unless it's actually the right call.** "The system was built in 2024" is fine. "Mistakes were made" is not.

### 2.2 Paragraph-level

- **One idea per paragraph.** First sentence states the idea. Following sentences support it.
- **First sentence is the most important.** It's the one a skimmer reads.
- **Last sentence bridges to the next section.** Each paragraph is a small argument.
- **Paragraph length: 2-4 sentences.** Long paragraphs are for long-form (case studies, blog). Short paragraphs for marketing pages.

### 2.3 Page-level

- **Headlines state the offer or the benefit.** Not "About Us": "A technology partner who thinks in systems, not features." Not "Services": "Three ways to start."
- **First paragraph answers the page's primary question.** If you only read the first sentence of each paragraph, you should understand the page.
- **Last paragraph is the CTA.** Not a "we hope you enjoyed" closer.

## 3. Headlines

### 3.1 The headline test

A good headline:

1. Names the value the visitor gets, OR
2. Names the problem the visitor has, OR
3. Promises a specific outcome.

A bad headline:

1. Describes the company ("Welcome to Elion").
2. Uses a buzzword ("Revolutionizing AI Operations").
3. Is too clever to parse ("Where Intelligence Meets Innovation").

### 3.2 Headline length

- **Hero headline: 6-10 words.** Punchy. Avoids period.
- **Section title: 3-6 words.** Direct.
- **Card title: 2-5 words.** Specific.
- **Blog post title: 8-14 words.** Descriptive enough to be findable in search.

### 3.3 Capitalization

- **Sentence case for headlines.** "Replace repetitive work with intelligent systems" (only "Replace" capitalized).
- **All-caps for labels and eyebrows.** "OUR APPROACH" in 12px uppercase letterspaced.
- **Title Case is forbidden.** "Replace Repetitive Work With Intelligent Systems" reads as marketing copy, not as conversation.

### 3.4 Hero headline candidates

For the V1 homepage, three options I'm proposing:

| Option | Headline                                      | Why                                                                                                         |
| ------ | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A      | "Same team. More output. Less friction."      | Three-beat rhythm. Memorable. Outcome-led. Technology is invisible.                                         |
| B      | "Grow without growing headcount."             | Punchy 4 words. Hits growth + headcount, but doesn't address 'freeing teams from repetitive work' directly. |
| C      | "More output from the team you already have." | Direct paraphrase of the positioning statement. Honest but slightly long for a hero.                        |

I recommend **A**. It's memorable, hits three of the brief's required outcomes (operational leverage, business growth, freeing teams from repetitive work), and avoids the AI vocabulary trap entirely.

**The hero headline and the positioning statement reinforce each other.** The headline is the homepage's expression of the positioning statement. Both should be true at the same time.

## 4. CTA Principles

### 4.1 The primary CTA

**"Book a Discovery Call"** is the primary CTA across the site.

Why this exact phrasing:

- **"Book"** is action-oriented, not vague.
- **"Discovery Call"** sets the expectation: this is a conversation, not a sales pitch.
- **"Call"** is specific. Not "chat" (could mean anything), not "session" (sounds formal), not "consultation" (sounds expensive).

### 4.2 Pricing questions route to the same CTA

Until pricing is validated, **every pricing question: wherever it appears: routes to "Book a Discovery Call."** No placeholder ranges, no "starts at" figures, no "$X,000+" copy.

Approved copy for pricing-inquiries:

> **Q: How much does a project cost?**
> **A:** Every engagement is scoped to fit the work. After the discovery call we send a short document outlining what we'd build, the approach, and the cost. If a fixed quote doesn't make sense for the work, we'll tell you that too.
>
> **[Book a Discovery Call →]**

This holds for the FAQ on every page, the About page, the Services pages, and any future blog post that asks about pricing.

### 4.3 Secondary CTAs

Used when the visitor isn't ready to book but wants more information:

- **"Explore Projects"**: homepage, services
- **"Read the case study"**: projects index → detail
- **"Learn more"**: service overview → detail
- **"See how it works"**: about → process

### 4.4 What CTAs are not

- ❌ "Get Started" (vague)
- ❌ "Contact Us" (corporate-speak)
- ❌ "Schedule Now" (sounds urgent in a bad way)
- ❌ "Free Consultation" (we don't say "free" because the work isn't free)
- ❌ "Learn More →" as a primary CTA (it's a weak action)
- ❌ "$X,000 starting price" (placeholder pricing in any form)

## 5. Messaging Rules

### 5.1 Always

- **State what you do in plain language.**
- **Name the specific outcome the visitor gets.**
- **Use the visitor's vocabulary, not yours.** They don't say "internal ops tooling": they say "the system we use to track [thing]."
- **Be specific. Numbers, durations, examples.**
- **Acknowledge what you don't do.** "We don't build marketing sites" is more credible than "We build everything."

### 5.2 Never

- ❌ Use "we" without a specific referent. "We help businesses" is vague. "We design internal systems for SMEs" is concrete.
- ❌ Use industry jargon unless the audience uses it. "Synergy," "leverage," "paradigm shift," "value-add": all forbidden.
- ❌ Use "AI-powered" as a description of something. AI is the means, not the feature. "An AI assistant" is fine. "AI-powered CRM" is marketing-speak.
- ❌ Use "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "next-generation," "disruptive," "innovative": unless they're literally true and the audience already knows it.
- ❌ Use "world-class," "best-in-class," "industry-leading": these are empty boasts.
- ❌ Use "just" in user-facing copy. "Just send an email" is patronizing.
- ❌ Use exclamation marks. They're almost always a substitute for confidence.

### 6.1 The "AI" rule in practice

The word "AI" is allowed in copy, but rarely needed. Default to one of these substitutes:

| Instead of...        | Try...                                  |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| AI automation        | Automation, workflows                   |
| AI assistant         | Internal assistant, knowledge assistant |
| AI-powered dashboard | Custom dashboard, intelligent dashboard |
| AI system            | System, software                        |
| AI features          | Capabilities, what it does              |

If a sentence reads naturally with "system" or "workflow" in place of "AI," use that. The visitor who actually cares that AI is involved will ask, and you can answer then.

## 7. Tone Examples

### 7.1 Same offer, three voices

**AI-agency-speak (avoid):**

> "Elion delivers cutting-edge AI solutions that empower enterprises to unlock transformative value across their operational ecosystems."

**Generic SaaS (avoid):**

> "We help businesses harness the power of AI to streamline operations and drive growth."

**Elion (use):**

> "We design automation, custom software, and AI systems that take repetitive work off your team's plate: so your people can focus on the work that actually matters."

The Elion version follows the messaging hierarchy: outcome first (repetitive work off your team's plate), solution second (we design systems), technology third (AI mentioned only in passing).

### 7.2 The "I'm a busy CEO" test

Read every paragraph aloud. If a CEO wouldn't finish the sentence, cut it.

> ❌ "Our comprehensive suite of AI-driven operational solutions leverages advanced machine learning algorithms to deliver transformative business outcomes across diverse industry verticals."
>
> ✅ "We design the systems your team needs to spend less time on repetitive work: and more time on the work that matters."

Same idea. The second version is half the length, ten times clearer, and never mentions AI.

### 7.3 Honest about uncertainty

Sometimes we don't know yet. That's fine.

> ❌ "We'll revolutionize your customer experience with AI."
>
> ✅ "We'll help you figure out where the work is: and where systems would actually move the needle: even if the answer turns out to be 'not yet'."

The second version is more credible because it's specific about what we're offering (clarity) and what we're not promising (a transformation).

### 7.4 The "technology partner" tone

When the visitor asks "what do you do?", we answer with the work, not the technology.

> ❌ "We're an AI Operations company that builds AI-powered systems for businesses."
>
> ✅ "We help growing businesses do more with the team they already have. We design the systems: automation, custom software, AI where it actually helps: that take the repetitive work off your team's plate."

The second version names the audience (growing businesses), the outcome (do more with the team they have), the work (we design the systems), and the technology (last, briefly). It never claims to be an "AI agency."

## 8. Grammar Standards

### 8.1 Punctuation

- **Oxford comma. Always.** "Automation, custom software, and AI systems" not "automation, custom software and AI systems."
- **One space after a period.** Not two.
- **Em dashes with spaces**: ": not hyphens: for parenthetical asides."
- **No semicolons unless they actually serve the sentence.**
- **No ellipses (...) for emphasis.** Use periods.
- **No emoji in production copy.** Never.

### 8.2 Numbers

- **Spell out one through nine.** "Three services" not "3 services."
- **Numerals for 10 and above.** "12 projects" not "twelve projects."
- **Numerals for any number with units.** "15 minutes" not "fifteen minutes."
- **Numerals for prices and percentages.** "$4,000" not "four thousand dollars." "20%" not "twenty percent."
- **Spell out at the start of a sentence.** "Twelve projects shipped in 2024."

### 8.3 Voice and tense

- **Use present tense for what's true now.** "We design systems."
- **Use past tense for what happened.** "We shipped the HR dashboard in March."
- **Avoid future tense in marketing copy.** "We will transform your business" sounds like a promise; "We design systems that..." sounds like a fact.

### 8.4 Contractions

**Use them.** "We're" not "we are." "Don't" not "do not." Contractions sound like a person. Not using them sounds like a press release.

## 9. Reading Level

### 9.1 Target

- **Grade level 8-10.** Roughly the level of a good newspaper.
- **Hemingway score: 8 or below.** Short sentences, few adverbs, no passive voice.

### 9.2 How to check

Use the Hemingway Editor (hemingwayapp.com) or a similar tool. If a paragraph scores above grade 10, simplify it.

### 9.3 Why this matters

- **Most of our audience reads at this level.** Even senior decision-makers prefer plain language: they have less time.
- **Plain language is faster to translate.** International visitors (Journey C in Phase 1) get a better experience.
- **Plain language signals competence.** A confident writer doesn't need jargon.

## 10. Templates

### 10.1 Service description (2-sentence template)

> We [specific action] for [specific audience] who [specific pain]. The result is [specific outcome] in [specific timeframe].

Example:

> We design internal assistants for growing teams who spend hours every week answering the same questions. Most teams save 5-10 hours a week within the first month.

### 10.2 Project description (problem → outcome)

> [Project type] built for [audience or self]. [Problem statement]. [Outcome or current state].

Example:

> Personal project: an internal CRM I built to manage client relationships for a recruitment firm. The team was tracking everything in spreadsheets and missing follow-ups. Now everything lives in one place with automatic reminders.

### 10.3 CTA strip (bottom of page)

> Ready to [specific outcome]? [Specific next step: "Book a 15-minute discovery call" / "Tell us about your project"].

Example:

> Ready to stop doing the same manual work every week? Book a 15-minute discovery call and we'll figure out where to start.
